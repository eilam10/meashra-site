The problem

Who vouches for an agent’s identity?

NHI vendors issue agent identities and enforce their access. But if there’s a security breach and a customer, insurer or auditor asks a CISO which agent acted and when its access was revoked, the answer comes from the company’s own NHI vendor. If that vendor’s records are disputed, unavailable or can’t be checked by anyone but the vendor, the CISO cannot independently prove which agent it was, so cannot establish who is liable or tell customers and regulators what was affected within legal deadlines.

Why Meashra

The trust layer your agents are missing

Built on open standards. Enforced at the identity layer.

Blockchain-anchored DIDs

Every agent gets a W3C Decentralized Identifier anchored on Ethereum. Portable, standards-based, cross-enterprise. Agent keys are designed for MPC threshold custody, so no single party, including us, can forge an identity record.

Verifiable by design

Registrations and revocations are public and checkable by anyone, with no account. Policy runs at the OIDC layer today; smart-contract enforcement is on the roadmap.

Real-time policy enforcement

Time-window policies gate every credential request at the OIDC layer, not after the fact.

Designed for audit evidence

Agent identity and revocation are recorded on-chain, so anyone can check them without relying on the vendor. Access events are designed to be anchored on-chain as a Merkle root.

Revocation on-chain

Revoke any agent on-chain in seconds. New credential issuance stops immediately; credentials already issued expire within their one-hour lifetime.

Enterprise role separation

CISO, DevOps and Security Engineer roles with distinct dashboard access, approval queues and governance controls.

Status: registry on testnet; MPC custody and anchored access events are the funded build.
